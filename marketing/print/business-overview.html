2. Service catalog — business notes

Customer-facing descriptions live on the website. The notes below are internal: demand drivers, revenue characteristics, and regulatory scope for planning.

Core

Companion care

Hourly, 4-hour minimum; weekly packages of 8, 12, or 20 hours. Recurring weekday visits create a predictable revenue base and a natural path to personal care as needs change.

Core

Personal care assistance

Highest-demand core service and the strongest driver of hours per client. Higher rate than companion care. Requires VDH HCO license, plan of care, training, supervision.

Core

Respite care

Bundled with personal care; standalone wellness-check rate. Supports family caregivers; strong referral channel from hospitals.

Core

Homemaker services

Light housekeeping, laundry, meal prep. Often the first service requested; lower margin but high retention.

Core

Transportation & errands

Non-clinical only. Do not perform construction, electrical, or medical work. Use licensed contractors for modifications.

Core

Medication reminders

Remind, prompt, document only — not administration. Premium hourly rate.

Core

Wellness check-ins

Bundled with personal care; standalone wellness-check rate. Low cost to deliver, high perceived value.

Growth

Dementia-friendly support

Premium hourly rate; 6-hour minimum for dementia visits. Specialized training required before launch.

Growth

Hospital-to-home bridge

Flat assessment fee, credited toward first month of care. Non-clinical support after discharge; coordinates with — never replaces — clinical team.

Overnight & 24-hour care

Highest revenue per client, but operationally demanding and margin-sensitive. Premium overnight rate; 24-hour care quoted as a daily package.

4. Differentiators — operational proof

Customer-facing versions of these appear on the website. The proof notes below are internal rationale.

One named care coordinator

Operational commitment. Staffing model must be funded before launch. Coordinator answers during office hours; documented on-call line after hours.

The Friday Family Brief

Differentiator built on a simple checklist and a shared inbox — low technical risk. Delivered by email, text link, or printed copy.

Caregiver matching

Reduces early cancellations, the most expensive failure point in home care. Fit checked at 72 hours and 14 days.

72-hour hospital-to-home bridge

Non-clinical only. Skilled nursing, therapy, wound care require separate licensure. Coordinates with — never replaces — discharging team.

A written quote, not a rate card

Transparency is a positioning choice. Rates are illustrative and need a margin review before go-live. No assessment fee; 4-hour minimum.

Bilingual care coordination

Hiring-dependent. Do not advertise languages until staff are verified. English and Spanish coordination for a county that is 26.8% Hispanic.

7. Virginia compliance & scope

  • Home care organization (HCO) license required for personal care (12VAC5-381). Application fee $1,500.
  • Companion/homemaker-only services may be exempt when provided alone (12VAC5-381-30).
  • Criminal record check before patient contact (12VAC5-381-110).
  • Insurance: ≥$1M general liability + ≥$50K crime/fidelity (12VAC5-381-210).
  • No clinical tasks: no skilled nursing, PT, OT, wound care, or medication administration.
  • Concept site does not claim an active license. Launch content must display the actual license number only after VDH issues it.

8. Internal positioning statement

Heart & Harbor Health & Medical Support is the communication-first home care agency for Prince William County: a named coordinator, a trained backup, a written quote before care begins, and a written family update every week. No clinical overreach. No long-term contract. No surprises on the invoice.
