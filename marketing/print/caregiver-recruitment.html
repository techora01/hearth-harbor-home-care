Now hiring · Prince William County

Caregiving is skilled work.
We treat it that way.

Consistent schedules. Real training. A supervisor who answers. And a team that does not pretend the job is easy.

What you will do

  • Help older adults stay in their own homes
  • Companionship, meals, errands, and light housekeeping
  • Personal care: bathing, dressing, grooming, transfers
  • Medication reminders — never administration
  • Document what happened, honestly, the same day
  • Call the coordinator when something changes

What we offer

  • Above-market hourly pay (placeholder range)
  • Consistent, published schedules — not on-call chaos
  • Paid orientation and personal care training
  • Supervision and a path to senior caregiver
  • Mileage reimbursement between clients
  • Paid time off that is actually honored
  • A named backup, so you can take a sick day

No experience? Apply anyway.

We will train the right person. What we cannot train is reliability, patience, and respect for someone else's home.

What we ask

  • A genuine interest in older adults
  • Reliability and clear communication
  • Willingness to learn and be supervised
  • Ability to pass a criminal records check
  • Two references we can actually reach
  • Legal authorization to work in the U.S.
  • A valid driver's license and reliable transportation

Before your first day

Virginia requires a State Police criminal record report and a sworn disclosure statement for home care employment. You cannot work with a client until the report is received. We pay for the process and walk you through it.

A realistic day

8:00 a.m. — Arrive. Knock, greet, check that the home is warm and safe. Start coffee. Help with a shower and dressing. Make breakfast. Sort the morning pills into the organizer the family set up. Sit and talk for twenty minutes. Note that she seemed more tired than last week. Call the coordinator before you leave. Write the visit note in the parking lot.
