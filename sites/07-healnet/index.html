Why we exist

Most families do not need more options. They need one reliable answer.

Home care in Northern Virginia is crowded, and much of it sounds identical. The difference shows up after the contract: whether the same caregiver arrives, whether the coordinator answers, and whether anyone tells you the truth when a plan stops working.

Heart & Harbor Medical Support is built around a narrower promise: a named coordinator, a trained backup, a written quote before care begins, and a written update every week. No clinical overreach. No long-term contract. No surprises on the invoice.

Questions

What families ask first.

Are you licensed?

This is a concept website. Heart & Harbor Medical Support does not yet hold a Virginia home care organization license. Personal care services in Virginia require licensure by the Virginia Department of Health, and the launch site must display the actual license number once issued. Companion and homemaker services can be offered without an HCO license when provided alone, but this business intends to be licensed before delivering personal care.

What is the difference between home care and home health?

Home care is professional help with daily living — companionship, meals, housekeeping, transportation, and personal care such as bathing and dressing. Home health is skilled clinical care ordered by a physician, such as nursing, physical therapy, or occupational therapy, and is delivered by a Medicare-certified home health agency. We provide home care and refer clinical needs to licensed providers.

How much does care cost?

We do not publish a single hourly rate because it would change with hours, schedule, and level of support. After a no-cost consultation we send an itemized written quote covering the hourly rate, any weekend or overnight premium, mileage, and the four-hour minimum. There is no assessment fee and no long-term contract.

Do you accept insurance, Medicare, or Medicaid?

No payment relationships are claimed on this concept site. Most professional home care in Virginia is paid privately, through long-term care insurance, or through veterans' benefits. Medicaid personal care is available through the CCC Plus waiver but requires DMAS enrollment and a provider agreement. Confirm coverage with the agency before making a decision.

Can you give my mother her medication?

Caregivers can remind, prompt, and document. Medication administration is a regulated nursing task and is outside our scope. We coordinate with the prescriber, pharmacy, and family, and we use a written medication list approved by the family.

What happens if our caregiver is sick?

Every client is assigned a named backup caregiver who has been introduced before an emergency. If both are unavailable, the coordinator covers the visit or arranges a qualified substitute. You receive a call, not a surprise.
