Legal & privacy

Privacy practices

A plain-language starting point for the privacy policy a licensed agency needs before collecting any health information.

Template, not legal advice

This is a demonstration privacy page. A launched agency must have counsel review its HIPAA analysis, business associate agreements, retention schedule, breach response plan, and state-law obligations.

What this demo site collects

No analytics, advertising pixels, or third-party trackers are installed. The contact form validates in the browser and does not transmit or store data. Fonts are loaded from Google Fonts, which receives the visitor's IP address as part of the request.

What a live agency would collect

  • Contact details and the reason for the inquiry
  • Care preferences, scheduling needs, and non-clinical assessment notes
  • Authorized family contacts and communication preferences
  • Billing information and service documentation

How information would be protected

Access limited to staff with a need to know; encrypted storage and transmission; unique user accounts; audit logging; written confidentiality policies; background-checked staff; and a documented breach response process. Protected health information would not be sent through ordinary text message or unencrypted email without authorization.

HIPAA status

Whether a home care agency is a HIPAA covered entity depends on its operations, including whether it conducts covered electronic transactions. Many private-pay companion agencies are not covered entities, but they may still be business associates of covered entities or subject to state privacy and consumer-protection law. This determination must be made with counsel — do not assume that “professional” means “outside HIPAA.”

Your choices

You can ask what information is held about you, request a correction, withdraw consent for non-essential communication, or ask for a copy of the agency's notice of privacy practices. Use the contact page to make a request.
