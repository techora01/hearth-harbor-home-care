# Customer Workflow — From First Call to Ongoing Care

**Prepared:** 23 November 2026
**Purpose:** Define the end-to-end client journey, the owner of each step, the required documentation, and the failure points to monitor.

---

## 1. Journey overview

```
Inquiry → Intake triage → Free consultation → Care plan → Service agreement
→ Caregiver matching → Introduction → First visit → 72-hour check
→ Weekly brief → 90-day reassessment → Ongoing / transition / discharge
```

Seven stages, thirteen handoffs, one accountable coordinator.

---

## 2. Stage 1 — Inquiry

**Trigger:** phone call, website form, referral from a hospital, attorney, or family.

**Owner:** Intake Coordinator (or administrator on duty).

**Standard:** live answer during office hours; return missed calls within **one business hour**; after-hours inquiries acknowledged by 9 a.m. the next business day.

**What happens:**
1. Greet warmly; let the caller tell the story.
2. Capture minimal contact information — name, phone, best time, community.
3. Do **not** collect detailed health information on an unsecured channel.
4. Triage urgency: is anyone unsafe right now? If yes, direct to 911 or Adult Protective Services.
5. Explain scope honestly: non-medical care; no nursing or therapy.
6. Offer a free 30-minute consultation.
7. Send a confirmation with the coordinator's name and time.

**Recorded in:** intake log (CRM or secure spreadsheet).

**Failure point:** the call goes to voicemail. Mitigation: an answering service or a shared on-call phone with a written script.

---

## 3. Stage 2 — Intake triage

**Owner:** Intake Coordinator.

**Purpose:** decide whether the agency can safely meet the need before investing an assessment.

**Decision tree:**

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Needs companion, homemaker, errands, respite | Proceed to consultation |
| Needs hands-on personal care and agency is licensed | Proceed, with RN review of the plan |
| Needs personal care but license is pending | Explain honestly; offer companion services only if lawful; refer to a licensed agency |
| Needs skilled nursing, wound care, IV, therapy | Refer to a Medicare-certified home health agency; offer non-clinical support alongside |
| Needs emergency medical help | 911 |
| Suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation | Adult Protective Services; follow mandated reporting |
| Needs 24/7 coverage and agency cannot staff it | Say so; refer |
| Outside service area | Say so immediately; offer resources |

**Rule:** a fast "we are not the right fit" is better than a slow maybe. It protects the family and the agency's reputation.

---

## 4. Stage 3 — Free consultation and non-clinical assessment

**Owner:** Care Coordinator, with RN consultation if personal care is likely.

**Format:** 45–60 minutes, in the home when possible, or video/phone if the family is remote.

**Agenda:**
1. Introductions and purpose; confirm no obligation.
2. The client's story: a typical day, what has changed, what is hard.
3. Functional picture: mobility, bathing, dressing, meals, medication, memory, mood, safety.
4. Home walkthrough: bathroom, bedroom, stairs, lighting, clutter, pets, exits.
5. Family system: who is involved, who decides, who pays, who lives nearby.
6. Preferences: language, gender, temperament, routines, cultural and religious practices.
7. Risk screen: falls, wandering, driving, nutrition, isolation, financial vulnerability.
8. Scope conversation: what we can do, what we cannot, and what needs a clinical referral.
9. Options: hours, schedule, rate, start date.
10. Next steps and a written summary.

**Outputs:**
- Non-clinical assessment summary
- Draft care plan
- Written quote with rate, hours, minimums, and premiums
- Referral list if clinical needs are identified

**Standard:** written summary within **48 hours**.

**Important:** this is a non-clinical assessment. It does not diagnose, treat, or replace a medical evaluation. If the assessor sees a medical concern, the response is a referral, not an opinion.

---

## 5. Stage 4 — Care plan

**Owner:** Care Coordinator; RN approval for personal care.

**Required contents:**
- Client identifying information and emergency contacts
- Goals in the client's words ("I want to stay in my own home," "I want to keep going to church")
- Tasks by day and time, with explicit scope boundaries
- Preferences: language, gender, routines, food, pets, cultural practices
- Risk factors and mitigation (fall risk, wandering, medication, infection)
- Communication plan (who gets the Friday brief, how)
- Backup caregiver plan
- Review date (90 days) and change process
- Signatures: client or responsible party, coordinator, RN if applicable

**Standard:** plan finalized before the first visit; reviewed at 90 days and after any significant change (hospitalization, fall, new diagnosis, caregiver change).

**Failure point:** a plan that lists tasks but not preferences. The caregiver then follows the tasks and ignores the person.

---

## 6. Stage 5 — Service agreement and financial setup

**Owner:** Administrator or designee.

**Documents:**
1. Service agreement (scope, exclusions, rates, minimums, premiums, cancellation, termination, privacy, complaints)
2. Financial agreement (billing cycle, payment method, late fees, returned-payment fee)
3. Consent for service
4. Authorization to share information (with named family, physician, insurer)
5. Notice of privacy practices
6. Client bill of rights
7. Complaint procedure
8. Emergency contact form

**Standard:** signed before the first billable visit. No verbal-only agreements.

**Plain-language check:** can a stressed family member explain the cancellation policy after one reading? If not, rewrite it.

---

## 7. Stage 6 — Caregiver matching and introduction

**Owner:** Scheduling Lead with the Care Coordinator.

**Matching criteria, in order:**
1. Clinical/functional competence (personal care, dementia experience, mobility)
2. Language
3. Gender preference, where lawful and appropriate
4. Temperament and interests
5. Schedule reliability and geography
6. Continuity (can this caregiver commit to the schedule?)

**Introduction visit:** 30–45 minutes. The primary caregiver and, where possible, the backup meet the client and family. The coordinator reviews the plan, answers questions, and confirms the first visit.

**Standard:** introduction before the first solo visit; backup introduced within 14 days.

**Failure point:** skipping the introduction to start billing sooner. It increases first-week cancellations.

---

## 8. Stage 7 — First visit

**Owner:** Assigned caregiver; Coordinator monitors.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Arrive on time; call if delayed by more than 10 minutes.
- [ ] Confirm identity and purpose at the door.
- [ ] Follow the plan; do not improvise clinical care.
- [ ] Document tasks, observations, food/fluid intake, mood, and any change.
- [ ] Report anything unusual to the coordinator before leaving.
- [ ] Leave the home secure.
- [ ] Complete the visit note the same day.

**Coordinator follow-up:** call the family within 24 hours. Ask three questions: Did the caregiver arrive on time? Did the visit match what you expected? Is there anything we should change?

---

## 9. Stage 8 — The first 72 hours

**Purpose:** the highest-risk window for cancellation and for missed clinical deterioration.

| Timing | Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Hour 24 | Family check-in call | Coordinator |
| Hour 48 | Caregiver debrief; adjust the plan if needed | Coordinator |
| Hour 72 | Fit check with client and family; rematch if needed | Coordinator |
| Day 7 | First Friday brief | Coordinator |
| Day 14 | Backup caregiver introduced; satisfaction pulse | Scheduling |

**Rematch policy:** if the fit is wrong, change the caregiver within 72 hours, no argument and no fee. A rematch is cheaper than a lost client.

---

## 10. Stage 9 — Ongoing service

**Weekly:** Friday Family Brief; schedule confirmation for the coming week; incident review.

**Monthly:** invoice and payment; caregiver supervision check; supply check; complaint log review.

**Quarterly:** 90-day reassessment; family check-in call; satisfaction survey; plan update.

**Annually:** full plan review; insurance and authorization updates; client rights re-acknowledgment.

**Change triggers:** hospitalization, fall, new diagnosis, medication change, caregiver departure, family conflict, financial change, or a client request.

---

## 11. Stage 10 — Discharge or transition

**Reasons:** client moves, enters a facility, needs exceed scope, passes away, requests termination, or the agency cannot safely staff.

**Standard:**
1. Notify in writing per the agreement (typically 7 days; immediate for safety).
2. Provide a written transition summary with the family's consent.
3. Return any client property promptly.
4. Final invoice within 7 days.
5. Offer a referral to an appropriate provider.
6. Record the reason and any lessons learned.

**Dignity rule:** a discharge is not a failure. It is a professional boundary. Families remember how it was handled.

## 12. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Owns | Key standard |
|---|---|---|
| Administrator | Compliance, licensure, insurance, staffing, finances | License current; audit calendar followed |
| Supervising RN | Personal care plans, competency, supervision | Plan approved before care; quarterly review |
| Care Coordinator | Relationship, plan, briefs, reassessment | 1-hour response; Friday brief; 90-day review |
| Scheduling Lead | Coverage, matching, callouts | Same-day coverage ≥95% within 2 hours |
| Intake Coordinator | First contact, triage, booking | Answer live; 1-hour callback |
| Caregiver | Direct care, documentation, observation | On time; same-day notes; scope discipline |
| Billing | Invoices, payments, insurance documentation | Accurate; on time; clear |

---

## 13. Documentation standards

Every visit note should answer:

1. Who was there and when?
2. What tasks were performed?
3. What did the caregiver observe (mood, appetite, mobility, skin, confusion)?
4. What changed from the plan?
5. What did the caregiver report, and to whom?

**Rules:** same-day entry; factual, not editorial; no speculation about diagnosis; no PHI in personal texts; secure storage; retention per policy.

## 14. Failure points and controls

| Failure | Early warning | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Unanswered phone | Missed-call log | Answering service; 1-hour callback standard |
| Slow assessment | Time from inquiry to visit | 48-hour scheduling target |
| Poor caregiver fit | 72-hour check feedback | Rematch within 72 hours |
| Missed visit | Scheduling flag | Backup bench; same-day coverage |
| Scope creep | Caregiver asks "can I just..." | Written task list; supervision; retraining |
| Billing dispute | First invoice questions | Written quote; itemized invoice; clear premiums |
| Complaint escalation | Repeated small complaints | Complaint log; 48-hour resolution; administrator review |
| Documentation gaps | Audit findings | 10% weekly audit; same-day notes |
| Family silence | No response to briefs | Quarterly call; alternate channel |
| Burnout | Callouts, lateness | Caseload limits; paid time off; recognition |

## 15. Technology requirements (minimum viable)

| Function | Minimum | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Intake and CRM | Secure spreadsheet or HIPAA-aware CRM | Do not use a personal inbox for PHI |
| Scheduling | Shared calendar with caregiver availability | Must handle recurring visits and callouts |
| Visit documentation | Mobile-friendly forms | Same-day entry; offline capability helpful |
| Secure messaging | HIPAA-aware app | No PHI in SMS |
| Billing | Invoicing and payment processing | Itemized; supports LTC insurance documentation |
| File storage | Encrypted, access-controlled | Retention and backup |
| Phone | Business line with voicemail and call tracking | After-hours script |

**Do not build custom software in year one.** Buy boring, proven tools and spend the money on caregivers and coordination.

## 16. Service-level targets

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Live answer during office hours | ≥90% |
| Callback for missed calls | ≤1 business hour |
| Inquiry to consultation | ≤48 hours |
| Consultation to first visit | ≤5 business days |
| On-time arrival (within 10 minutes) | ≥95% |
| Same-day callout coverage | ≥95% within 2 hours |
| Friday brief on time | ≥98% |
| 90-day retention | ≥85% |
| Complaint resolution | ≤48 hours |
| Care plan review on schedule | 100% |
