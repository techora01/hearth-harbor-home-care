# Competitive Differentiators — Evaluation & Recommendation

**Prepared:** 23 November 2026
**Purpose:** Separate differentiators that are commercially real from those that only sound good in a pitch deck.

---

## 1. Evaluation method

Each candidate is scored 1–5 on:

- **Demand** — how often families ask for it or switch agencies because of it.
- **Margin** — whether it supports price or retention without eroding gross margin.
- **Operability** — how hard it is to deliver consistently with a small team.
- **Regulatory safety** — lower risk of exceeding Virginia scope or creating liability.
- **Defensibility** — how hard it is for a competitor to copy within 90 days.

Scores are planning judgments, not measured data. A high total is necessary but not sufficient; the business should only adopt what it can staff and supervise.

## 2. Primary positioning: "communication-first"

**Chosen primary differentiator:** a named coordinator + a written weekly family brief + published rates.

**Why this combination, not one item:** any single feature is copyable. The combination creates an operating system:

- The coordinator owns the relationship.
- The Friday brief forces documentation discipline.
- Published rates remove the most common pre-sale friction.

Together they answer the three questions families actually have: *Who is responsible? What happened? What will this cost?*

**Cost to deliver (year one):** roughly 3–5 coordinator hours per client per month, plus a simple shared inbox and a template. No custom software required. The demo sites show what the family-facing output could look like; a real launch can start with email + a PDF template.

**Failure mode:** the brief becomes a generic checklist no one reads. Mitigation: keep it under 150 words, lead with one meaningful observation, and audit 10% of briefs weekly for specificity.

## 3. Supporting differentiators

### 3.1 Caregiver matching and a named backup

**What it is:** a structured intake of language, temperament, routine, pets, and interests; a primary caregiver; an introduced backup; a 72-hour fit check and a 14-day review.

**Why it matters:** early churn is usually a fit problem, not a competence problem. A rematch is cheaper than losing the client.

**Operational requirement:** a bench of at least 1.3 caregivers per active client, or a part-time flex pool. Do not promise a named backup before the bench exists.

**Risk:** over-promising consistency while running thin. Publish the policy only when staffing supports it.

### 3.2 72-hour hospital-to-home bridge

**What it is:** non-clinical support for the first three days home: ride, prescriptions, groceries, meal setup, fall-hazard clearing, medication reminder setup, 24/72-hour calls, written summary.

**Why it matters:** discharge is a high-urgency, high-referral moment. Medicare covers skilled home health but not custodial or companion care [S22]. The bridge fills the gap without competing with clinical providers.

**Regulatory boundary:** no clinical assessment, no wound care, no medication administration, no interference with the home health plan. A written coordination protocol and a HIPAA-aware information-handling process are required.

**Revenue:** $395 flat package (illustrative), with optional ongoing care. Referral partners like a fixed, easy-to-explain offer.

### 3.3 Bilingual coordination

**What it is:** intake, care-plan review, and family updates in English and Spanish when a qualified coordinator is on staff.

**Why it matters:** 26.8% of county residents identify as Hispanic or Latino [S2]. Machine translation is not acceptable for care instructions.

**Operational requirement:** hire and verify bilingual staff before advertising the capability. Budget for translated print materials and a language-access policy. Do not use a caregiver as an unpaid interpreter during clinical conversations.

**Risk:** advertising a language capability the agency cannot consistently staff. This is a credibility cliff; launch only when coverage is real.

### 3.4 Home safety and aging-in-place review

**What it is:** a room-by-room, non-clinical walkthrough with a prioritized written report and cost ranges.

**Why it matters:** it is a low-commitment paid entry offer that demonstrates expertise. It also reduces fall risk, which protects the client and the agency.

**Limits:** no construction, electrical, or medical work. Refer licensed contractors for modifications. Never imply that a safety review prevents all falls.

### 3.5 Family caregiver coaching

**What it is:** a 90-minute paid planning session with a written action plan, local resource map, and optional 30-day check-in.

**Why it matters:** it builds trust with families who are not ready for ongoing care and creates a pipeline. It also supports the respite service line.

**Limits:** education and planning only. No legal, financial, or clinical advice. Refer to elder law attorneys and clinicians.

## 4. Deferred or rejected ideas

| Idea | Why not now |
|---|---|
| **Custom family portal / app** | High build and support cost, low adoption among older adults, and a distraction from the human promise. Buy an off-the-shelf HIPAA-aware portal later if clients ask. |
| **Medication administration** | Regulated nursing task. Requires clinical licensure, supervision, and liability exposure. Reminders only. |
| **Skilled nursing / therapy** | Requires Medicare certification, clinical leadership, and a different operating model. Partner instead. |
| **24/7 continuous care at launch** | Staffing intensity, fatigue risk, and thin margins. Add after 12 months with a proven overnight bench. |
| **Dementia "memory care"** | The phrase implies a clinical program. Offer dementia-friendly non-clinical support with specialized training, not memory care. |
| **Transportation as a profit center** | High demand but low margin after vehicle, insurance, and wait time. Bundle it; do not build the brand on it. |
| **Per-referral payments** | Fraud-and-abuse and inducement risk. Use education and reciprocity instead. |
| **Celebrity or stock testimonials** | Fabricated social proof destroys trust and may violate consumer-protection rules. Publish verified reviews only, with consent. |

## 5. The moat

A competitor can copy a tagline in a day. Copying this operating model requires:

1. Hiring coordinators with enough capacity to own 35–45 clients each.
2. Building a caregiver bench deep enough to guarantee a named backup.
3. Enforcing a weekly documentation rhythm.
4. Publishing rates and accepting the pricing discipline that follows.
5. Training staff to say "we don't do that" without losing the relationship.

That is unglamorous work. It is also why the position is defensible.

## 6. Proof plan — how to validate before scaling

| Claim | Test | Success threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Families value the weekly brief | Offer it to first 10 clients; survey at 30 days | ≥70% read it; ≥50% say it increased trust |
| Published rates increase conversion | A/B landing page with vs. without rates | ≥15% lift in qualified inquiries |
| Named backup reduces churn | Track 90-day retention for matched vs. unmatched | ≥10-point improvement |
| Hospital bridge drives referrals | Track source for every bridge client | ≥3 repeat referral sources in 90 days |
| Bilingual coordination expands reach | Spanish landing page + community event | ≥10 qualified Spanish-language inquiries in 60 days |

If a claim fails its test, remove it from the website. Positioning must follow evidence.

---

## 7. Recommendation summary

**Primary:** communication-first care — named coordinator, weekly written brief, published rates.
**Supporting:** caregiver matching + named backup, 72-hour hospital bridge, bilingual coordination (when staffed), home safety review, family coaching.
**Later:** dementia-friendly specialization, overnight/24-hour care, Medicaid contracting, a purchased family portal.
**Never:** medication administration, skilled clinical services, fabricated testimonials, per-referral kickbacks.
