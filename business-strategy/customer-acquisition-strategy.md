# Customer Acquisition Strategy — First 100 Clients

**Prepared:** 23 November 2026
**Budget assumption:** $3,000–$6,000 per month for the first 6 months, excluding payroll.
**Goal:** 25–40 active clients within 12–18 months, at a blended $34–$36/hour and 12–16 hours/week per client.

---

## 1. The acquisition thesis

Home care is not an impulse purchase. It is triggered by an event — a fall, a hospital discharge, a diagnosis, a caregiver collapse — and the buyer is usually an adult child in crisis, searching on a phone at night.

That means:

1. **Search intent is high and urgent.** Local SEO and a fast, clear website matter more than brand advertising.
2. **Referrals from trusted professionals outperform ads.** Discharge planners, elder law attorneys, and primary care offices send families who are already convinced.
3. **Trust is built before the first call.** Published rates, scope honesty, and a real address do more than a stock photo of a smiling caregiver.
4. **Speed-to-contact wins.** A family that calls three agencies books the one that answers.
5. **Retention is acquisition.** A referred family that stays two years is worth more than five one-off leads.

## 3. Channel playbooks

### 3.1 Google Business Profile and local SEO

**Why first:** the highest-intent search in this category is "home care near me" or "home care Woodbridge VA," often on a phone, often at night.

**Actions:**
- Create and verify a Google Business Profile with the real (or placeholder, pre-launch) address, service area, hours, phone, and 8–10 photos.
- Choose the primary category "Home Health Care Service" and secondary categories that accurately reflect non-medical scope.
- Publish 24+ service and location pages (already built in the demo sites).
- Collect reviews from real clients with written consent; respond to every review.
- Post weekly updates: care tips, local events, staff training — not sales.
- Build local citations: Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Nextdoor, local chambers.
- Ensure NAP (name, address, phone) consistency everywhere.

**Target:** top-3 local pack for "home care [community]" in 3–5 communities within 9 months.

**Measurement:** calls, direction requests, website clicks, form fills by source.

---

### 3.2 Hospital and rehabilitation discharge referrals

**Why:** discharge is the highest-urgency moment. Medicare covers skilled home health but not custodial or companion care [S22], so families need a non-clinical option immediately.

**Targets:** Sentara Northern Virginia Medical Center (Woodbridge), UVA Health Prince William Medical Center (Manassas), Novant Health Haymarket Medical Center, Inova facilities serving the western county, and local skilled nursing/rehab facilities.

**Actions:**
- Build a one-page scope sheet: what we do, what we do not do, response time, rates, and a single intake number.
- Offer the 72-hour hospital-to-home bridge as a fixed, easy-to-explain package.
- Meet discharge planners and case managers in person; bring coffee, not a contract.
- Ask about their workflow: when do they identify non-clinical needs, and what makes a referral easy?
- Provide a same-day triage promise during business hours.
- Never ask for or accept per-referral payment.
- Track every referral source and close the loop with the planner (within privacy limits).

**Target:** 3–5 active discharge-planner relationships within 6 months; 30% of new clients from clinical referrals by month 12.

---

### 3.3 Elder law attorneys and financial planners

**Why:** they meet families before the crisis, during estate and long-term-care planning. They are asked "where do I start?" and they refer people they trust.

**Actions:**
- Build a referral card and a short "planning for care" guide.
- Offer a free 30-minute educational talk for their client events.
- Be responsive and never oversell; attorneys refer reliability, not marketing.
- Clarify that you do not give legal or financial advice.
- Co-host a "paying for care" workshop with a local attorney.

**Target:** 4–6 attorney relationships; 10–15% of new clients by month 12.

---

### 3.4 Senior centers and community events

**Why:** trusted local touchpoints with direct access to older adults and caregivers.

**Targets:** Prince William Area Agency on Aging senior centers, Manassas senior center, church groups, libraries, and cultural associations.

**Actions:**
- Offer educational talks: "When a parent needs help," "Home safety myths," "What Medicare does not cover."
- Bring printed resource maps, not brochures about yourself.
- Sponsor a caregiver support group's refreshments (modest, disclosed).
- Follow up with attendees who ask questions; do not harvest contact lists.
- Partner with the Area Agency on Aging as a private-pay resource.

**Target:** 2 events per month; 20–30 qualified conversations per quarter.

---

### 3.5 Paid search

**Why:** captures urgent intent when organic is not yet ranking.

**Approach:**
- Start with a tight geo radius around Woodbridge, Manassas, and Gainesville.
- Bid on high-intent terms: "home care Woodbridge VA," "elderly care Manassas," "respite care Prince William County," "24 hour home care" only if you actually offer it.
- Send traffic to the matching service or location page, not the homepage.
- Use call-only ads during office hours and a form after hours.
- Negative keywords: jobs, salary, training, free, CNA classes, volunteer.
- Track calls with a dedicated number.

**Budget:** $1,000–$2,500/month. Expect $40–$120 per qualified lead; a closed client at $22,500/year justifies a high CAC.

**Guardrail:** do not outbid your ability to answer the phone. A paid click to an unanswered line is a wasted dollar.

---

### 3.6 Facebook and Nextdoor community groups

**Why:** local caregiver questions appear daily in community groups.

**Approach:**
- Join local groups as a person, not a billboard; read the rules.
- Answer questions helpfully without pitching.
- Share educational articles and the resource map.
- Never solicit reviews or post in groups that prohibit business promotion.
- Run modest geo-targeted ads to the location pages for adult children 40–65.

**Target:** 5–10 inbound conversations per month by month 6.

---

### 3.7 Spanish-language outreach

**Why:** 26.8% of county residents identify as Hispanic or Latino [S2]. Language access is a real market requirement, not a diversity checkbox.

**Approach:**
- Hire a bilingual coordinator **before** advertising Spanish-language service.
- Translate the website, service agreement, and care plan with a professional translator.
- Build relationships with Spanish-speaking faith communities, tiendas, and community organizations.
- Run Spanish-language workshops on caring for aging parents.
- Ensure phone intake can actually handle a Spanish-language call.

**Guardrail:** a Spanish landing page with no Spanish-speaking staff is worse than no page at all.

---

### 3.8 Long-term care insurance agents

**Why:** policyholders need a provider who can document care for reimbursement.

**Approach:**
- Build a documentation packet: care plan, visit notes, invoices, and a reimbursement summary.
- Ask agents what they need to make a claim smooth.
- Do not promise coverage; the insurer decides.

## 4. The conversion path

```
Awareness → Trust → Contact → Assessment → First visit → Retention → Referral
```

| Stage | Owner | Standard | Metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Marketing | Local pages indexed; GBP active; 2 events/month | Impressions, calls |
| Trust | Website | Rates, scope, coordinator, backup visible | Time on page, form starts |
| Contact | Intake | Answer live during hours; return calls in 1 business hour | Speed to lead |
| Assessment | Coordinator | Free, 45–60 min, written plan within 48 hours | Assessment-to-close rate |
| First visit | Scheduling | Caregiver + backup introduced; 72-hour check | First-week retention |
| Retention | Coordinator | Friday brief; quarterly call; 90-day reassessment | 90-day retention ≥85% |
| Referral | Everyone | Ask at 90 days if they would refer; make it easy | Referral rate ≥20% |

## 5. Intake script (phone)

**Answer within three rings. Smile before speaking.**

1. "Thank you for calling Hearth & Harbor. This is [name]. How can I help?"
2. Listen without interrupting. Let them tell the story.
3. "I'm sorry — that sounds like a lot. Can I ask a few questions so I can tell you honestly whether we can help?"
4. Gather: who needs care, what is happening, where they live, what they need help with, timeline, who is deciding, budget awareness.
5. "Here's what we can do, and here's what we don't do." Be specific about non-medical scope.
6. "Our rates are published, and the first conversation is free. Would [time] work for a 30-minute visit?"
7. Send a confirmation text/email with the coordinator's name and the time.
8. Log the call and set a follow-up task.

**Never:** promise clinical care, quote a rate you cannot honor, pressure for a same-day decision, or ask for sensitive health details before a secure channel is established.

## 6. Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "We can't afford it." | "Let's look at what you actually need. Twelve hours a week is often enough to prevent a crisis. We can also talk about long-term care insurance and VA benefits." |
| "We'll wait until something happens." | "That's common. A home safety review now is cheaper than a fall. And you'll have our number when you need it." |
| "I don't want strangers in the house." | "That's the right instinct. You'll meet the caregiver and a backup before care starts, and we'll change the match if it isn't working." |
| "Medicare covers this, right?" | "Medicare covers short-term skilled home health ordered by a doctor. It generally does not cover companion or custodial care. I'll explain what your policy actually covers." |
| "You're new." | "We are. Ask for our license status, insurance certificate, background-check policy, and a sample care plan. We'll send all four." |
| "I need to talk to my siblings." | "Of course. I'll send a one-page summary you can share, and we can do a family call." |

## 10. What not to do

- **Do not buy leads from aggregators at launch.** They are expensive, shared with competitors, and erode your positioning.
- **Do not pay for referrals.** Fraud-and-abuse and inducement risk.
- **Do not advertise 24/7, dementia care, or nursing before you can deliver it.**
- **Do not use stock photos of caregivers you did not employ.**
- **Do not promise "the same caregiver every time" without a backup bench.**
- **Do not run broad brand ads before conversion is working.** Fix the funnel first.
- **Do not ignore the phone.** The single biggest leak in home care marketing is an unanswered call.
