# Business Launch Roadmap — 18 Months

**Prepared:** 23 November 2026
**Business:** Hearth & Harbor Home Care (concept)
**Target market:** Prince William County, Virginia
**Model:** Licensed home care organization (personal care) with companion, homemaker, and respite core services

> **This is a planning document, not a forecast.** Every financial figure is an assumption built from public benchmarks. Validate with a bottom-up cost model, three insurance quotes, and a CPA before committing capital.

## 3. Phase 1 — Build (months 2–5)

### Critical path: licensure

The VDH license is the long pole. Everything else can proceed in parallel, but no personal care may be delivered until the license is issued.

**Application package** (see `virginia-compliance-checklist.md` for the full list):
- Application and $1,500 fee
- Organizational chart
- Policies and procedures manual
- Sample care plan, client record, service agreement
- Insurance certificates
- Administrator qualifications
- RN agreement and qualifications
- Training and competency records
- Criminal records check policy
- Office documentation

**Realistic timeline:** 60–120 days after a complete submission, plus time to correct deficiencies. Plan for 6 months from decision to license in hand.

### Hiring plan

| Role | When | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Administrator | Month 2 | Full-time | Must meet 12VAC5-381-170; may be the owner if qualified |
| Supervising RN | Month 2 | Contract, 4–10 hrs/week | Personal care plans, competency, supervision |
| Care Coordinator | Month 3 | Full-time | Intake, plans, Friday briefs, family relationships |
| Scheduling Lead | Month 4 | Full-time or combined | Coverage, matching, callouts |
| Caregivers | Month 4–5 | 4–6 to start | Employees, not contractors; background checks before client contact |
| Billing / admin | Month 5 | Part-time | Invoicing, LTC insurance documentation |

**Compensation assumptions (planning, not verified):**
- Caregiver: $18–$21/hour plus burden
- Coordinator: $52,000–$65,000/year
- Administrator: $75,000–$95,000/year
- RN: $55–$85/hour contract

**Recruiting:** indeed, Care.com (for reach, not for hiring contractors), local community colleges, church networks, caregiver referral bonuses (paid on hire and retention, not per client referral), and Spanish-language job boards.

### Systems

| System | Choice | Monthly |
|---|---|---|

| Phone | Business VoIP with call tracking | $60–$120 |

## 4. Phase 2 — Launch (months 5–8)

### Goals
- 10 active clients by month 8.
- 90-day retention ≥85%.
- Zero compliance incidents.
- At least 3 repeat referral sources.

### Sequence

**Month 5**
- License issued; insurance bound; final walkthrough with RN.
- First two caregivers fully onboarded and competency-validated.
- Website live with real contact details and verified license number.
- Google Business Profile verified; citations built.
- Discharge planner visits begin (5 in the first month).

**Month 6**
- First 3–5 clients. Founder/administrator personally attends the first visit of every client.
- Friday brief goes out every week without exception.
- 72-hour check on every new client.
- Weekly team huddle: what broke, what is at risk, what to fix.

**Month 7**
- 6–8 clients. First caregiver backup test (simulate a callout).
- First elder law attorney lunch-and-learn.
- First senior center talk.
- Paid search begins with a tight budget.

**Month 8**
- 10 clients. First 90-day reassessment.
- First satisfaction survey.
- Referral source review: double down on what produced clients; cut what did not.

### Launch metrics

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Active clients | 10 |
| Average hours/week | 12–16 |
| Gross margin | ≥25% |
| 90-day retention | ≥85% |
| On-time arrival | ≥95% |
| Friday brief on time | ≥98% |
| Complaints | ≤2, all resolved ≤48 hours |
| Referral sources | ≥3 repeat |

## 5. Phase 3 — Stabilize (months 8–12)

### Goals
- 25 active clients.
- Gross margin ≥28%.
- Backup bench covers 95% of callouts within 2 hours.
- Phase 2 services launched (hospital bridge, home safety review).
- Second coordinator hired or in training.

### Priorities

1. **Retention over acquisition.** A lost client costs ~$22,500/year. Fix fit problems at 72 hours.
2. **Backup bench.** Hire ahead of demand; 1.3 caregivers per active client is the planning ratio.
3. **Documentation discipline.** 10% weekly audit of care notes and briefs.
4. **Referral depth.** Convert one-off referral sources into recurring relationships.
5. **Pricing review.** Validate the rate against actual cost; adjust with notice.
6. **Bilingual capacity.** Hire a bilingual coordinator before advertising Spanish service.

### Risks at this stage

| Risk | Signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Coordinator overload | Briefs late; calls missed | Hire second coordinator at 35 clients |
| Caregiver turnover | Callouts rising | Raise pay; improve scheduling consistency; exit interview |
| Margin erosion | Overtime, travel, unbilled time | Route optimization; 4-hour minimum enforcement |
| Scope creep | Caregivers "helping" with meds | Retraining; task-list audit; supervision |
| Reputation incident | Complaint trend | Root-cause review; transparency with family |

## 6. Phase 4 — Expand (months 12–18)

### Goals
- 35–45 active clients.
- Positive operating cash flow.
- Phase 3 evaluation (overnight care) with a staffing bench.
- Decision on Medicaid CCC Plus enrollment.
- A second coordinator and a defined career ladder for caregivers.

### Expansion rules

1. **Do not expand geography before operations are stable.** Ten communities is enough.
2. **Do not add overnight care without four trained overnight caregivers.**
3. **Do not enroll in Medicaid without a billing and compliance plan.** Lower reimbursement requires volume.
4. **Do not open a second office.** A single licensed location with a strong coordinator model is more efficient.
5. **Do not chase facility contracts** until the private-pay core is profitable.

## 9. Financial model (planning assumptions)

### 9.1 Revenue per client

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Average hours/week | 14 |
| Blended rate | $34/hour |
| Weekly revenue | $476 |
| Annual revenue | $24,752 |

### 9.2 Cost per billable hour

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Caregiver wage | $19.50 |
| Payroll burden (FICA, FUTA, SUTA, workers' comp) | 22% → $4.29 |
| Supervision and coordination allocation | $5.00 |
| Travel and mileage | $1.50 |
| Supplies and PPE | $0.60 |
| **Total direct cost** | **$30.89** |
| **Gross margin per hour** | **$3.11 (9%)** |

**This is the critical finding.** At a $34 rate and $19.50 wage, the margin is thin. The business only works if:

1. The blended rate is **$36–$38**, or
2. The caregiver wage is **$17–$18** with strong retention, or
3. Utilization is high (few unbilled hours), or
4. Coordination is leveraged across 35–45 clients.

### 9.3 Revised target model

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Blended rate | $36 |
| Caregiver wage | $19.00 |
| Burden (22%) | $4.18 |
| Supervision/coordination | $4.50 |
| Travel | $1.40 |
| Supplies | $0.50 |
| **Direct cost** | **$29.58** |
| **Gross margin** | **$6.42/hour (17.8%)** |

At 40 clients × 14 hours × 52 weeks × $6.42 = **$186,950 annual gross profit**. Subtract overhead:

| Overhead | Annual |
|---|---|
| Administrator | $85,000 |
| Two coordinators | $120,000 |
| Scheduling/billing | $55,000 |
| RN contract | $20,000 |
| Office | $18,000 |
| Insurance | $14,000 |
| Software | $5,000 |
| Marketing | $24,000 |
| Professional fees | $10,000 |
| **Total overhead** | **$351,000** |

**Result: a loss at 40 clients.** The model requires either 60+ clients, a higher rate, lower overhead, or a leaner staffing mix at launch.

### 9.4 What this means

1. **Do not underprice.** $32/hour cannot support a licensed, insured, supervised agency. Publish $36–$38 for personal care.
2. **Control overhead.** The administrator may need to also coordinate at launch. The second coordinator comes at 35 clients, not before.
3. **Watch utilization.** Unbilled travel and gaps destroy margin.
4. **Respite and companion care carry the margin.** Personal care is the loss leader that wins referrals.
5. **Model before launch, not after.** The demo pricing is illustrative precisely because this math is unforgiving.

**Recommendation:** build the financial model in a spreadsheet with real quotes before signing a lease. The numbers above are a warning, not a business plan.

## 10. Launch gate (repeat from compliance)

Do not accept the first client until:

- [ ] License issued (or exemption granted).
- [ ] Insurance bound at required limits.
- [ ] Administrator qualified and documented.
- [ ] RN agreement signed.
- [ ] Criminal records checks complete for every caregiver with client contact.
- [ ] Policies adopted and staff trained.
- [ ] Service agreement, care plan, privacy notice, and complaint procedure finalized.
- [ ] Scheduling, billing, and after-hours coverage operational.
- [ ] Website claims match reality.
- [ ] Two caregivers competency-validated.
- [ ] One referral partner briefed.
- [ ] Emergency protocol tested.

## 13. What success looks like

Not a hundred clients. Not a franchise. A small, profitable, trusted agency where:

- Families know who is coming and what happened.
- Caregivers stay more than a year.
- The phone is answered.
- The license is clean.
- The founder can take a week off without the operation collapsing.

That is the business the nine websites are designed to represent.
