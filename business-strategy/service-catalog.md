# Service Catalog — Heart & Harbor Medical Support

**Prepared:** 23 November 2026
**Status:** Launch catalog + phased roadmap. All prices illustrative until a bottom-up cost model is complete.
**Scope rule:** Every service below is **professional**. No skilled nursing, physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech therapy, wound care, or medication administration is offered or implied.

---

## 1. Catalog design principles

1. **Fewer services, delivered well.** A new agency that offers twelve things badly loses to one that offers five things reliably.
2. **Nothing clinical.** Clinical scope requires a different license, clinical leadership, and a different insurance program. We partner instead.
3. **Every service has a written task list.** "Companion care" means nothing until the caregiver and the family can see the tasks.
4. **Every service has a price and a minimum.** No "call for pricing."
5. **Phased additions are gated.** A service is added only when staffing, training, supervision, and insurance exist.

## 2. Launch services (Phase 1)

### 2.1 Companion care

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Slug** | `companion-care` |
| **Who it is for** | Older adults living alone or with a spouse; adults with limited mobility; anyone at risk of isolation |
| **Core tasks** | Conversation and companionship; shared meals and coffee; walks; games, puzzles, reading aloud; accompanying to appointments and social events; help with correspondence and phone calls; observing and reporting changes |
| **Explicitly excluded** | Bathing, dressing, toileting, transfers, medication administration, clinical assessment |
| **Minimum** | 4 hours per visit |
| **Rate** | $32/hour (illustrative) |
| **Delivery model** | 1 caregiver : 1 client; 4–40 hours/week typical |
| **Margin profile** | Highest gross margin in the catalog; low supply cost |
| **Training required** | Company orientation, infection control, confidentiality, mandated reporting, emergency procedures, dementia communication basics |

### 2.2 Personal care assistance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Slug** | `personal-care` |
| **Who it is for** | Adults who need hands-on help with activities of daily living (ADLs) |
| **Core tasks** | Bathing and showering; dressing and grooming; toileting and incontinence care; oral hygiene; hair and skin care; feeding assistance; safe transfers with gait belt or standby assistance; mobility support; bed positioning |
| **Explicitly excluded** | Wound care, catheter care, ostomy care, injections, medication administration, clinical monitoring |
| **Minimum** | 4 hours per visit |
| **Rate** | $36/hour (illustrative) |
| **Regulatory note** | Personal care is a licensed home care organization (HCO) service in Virginia [S5][S6]. **Do not deliver this service before the VDH license is issued.** |
| **Training required** | All companion training plus a documented personal care aide curriculum, competency validation, and supervision by the qualified RN supervisor |
| **Margin profile** | Lower than companion care because of training, supervision, and workers' compensation exposure |

### 2.3 Respite care

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Slug** | `respite-care` |
| **Who it is for** | Spouses and adult children providing full-time unpaid care |
| **Core tasks** | Stand-in coverage so the family caregiver can work, travel, sleep, or attend to their own health; continuity of the established routine; written handover notes |
| **Minimum** | 4 hours per visit |
| **Rate** | $34/hour (illustrative) |
| **Delivery model** | Scheduled blocks (4, 8, or 12 hours); recurring weekly slots are the highest-retention product |
| **Why it matters locally** | AARP Virginia values unpaid family care at about $27 billion per year [S12]. Respite is the lowest-commitment entry point and the strongest retention product. |
| **Margin profile** | Similar to companion care; premium for weekend and holiday blocks |

### 2.4 Homemaker & light housekeeping

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Slug** | `homemaker-services` |
| **Who it is for** | Clients whose home has become difficult to maintain; families preparing a home for a return from hospital |
| **Core tasks** | Light cleaning; laundry and linen changes; dishes; meal preparation and batch cooking; grocery list and pantry organization; pet care basics; mail sorting; changing light bulbs; decluttering pathways |
| **Explicitly excluded** | Heavy cleaning, yard work, home repair, pest control, construction, moving furniture over safe limits |
| **Minimum** | 4 hours per visit |
| **Rate** | $32/hour (illustrative) |
| **Regulatory note** | Homemaker and chore services alone can fall outside HCO licensure under 12VAC5-381-30 [S6]. However, if the same agency also provides personal care, the licensed entity and its supervision standards apply. Do not use the exemption to avoid licensure while delivering personal care. |
| **Margin profile** | Moderate; supply costs (cleaning products) are minor but must be itemized |

### 2.5 Transportation & errands

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Slug** | `transportation-errands` |
| **Who it is for** | Clients who no longer drive, or whose family cannot provide weekday transport |
| **Core tasks** | Rides to medical and dental appointments (non-emergency); pharmacy pickup; grocery shopping; bank and post office; social and religious events; waiting-room accompaniment; reporting back to family |
| **Explicitly excluded** | Emergency transport, ambulance, wheelchair-lift transport unless the vehicle and driver are equipped and insured for it, transporting clients who require clinical monitoring |
| **Minimum** | 4 hours per visit (includes travel and wait time) |
| **Rate** | $32/hour plus mileage at the IRS rate, capped and disclosed |
| **Risk controls** | Personal auto policy must cover business use; non-owned vehicle endorsement; seat belt policy; no transporting clients in a caregiver's personal vehicle without written approval and insurance verification |
| **Margin profile** | Low. Treat as a retention and referral service, not a profit center. |

### 2.6 Medication reminders

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Slug** | `medication-reminders` |
| **Who it is for** | Clients who forget doses but self-administer |
| **Core tasks** | Verbal and visual reminder at the scheduled time; confirm the client took the dose; document the time; report missed doses or side effects to the family and, with authorization, the prescriber; maintain a medication list supplied by the family or pharmacy |
| **Explicitly excluded** | Administering medication, pouring or pre-pouring pills, injecting, applying medicated creams, adjusting doses, advising on medications, disposing of controlled substances |
| **Minimum** | Included in a care visit; not sold alone |
| **Rate** | Included in the hourly rate |
| **Regulatory note** | Medication administration is a regulated nursing task in Virginia. Reminders and documentation are not administration, but the line must be trained and audited. When in doubt, do not touch the pills. |
| **Margin profile** | No incremental revenue; high retention value and risk reduction |

### 2.7 Wellness check-ins

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Slug** | `wellness-check-ins` |
| **Who it is for** | Independent older adults whose family lives far away; clients between larger care episodes |
| **Core tasks** | Scheduled visit or call; wellbeing and safety observation; hydration and nutrition check; home hazard spot-check; medication reminder; written report to the family; escalation if something is wrong |
| **Minimum** | 1-hour visit (the only sub-4-hour product, because no personal care is delivered) |
| **Rate** | $45 per visit (illustrative) or bundled |
| **Delivery model** | 1–3 visits per week; a low-cost way to keep a client engaged until needs increase |
| **Margin profile** | Moderate; short visits carry travel overhead, so cluster geographically |
| **Note** | This is not a medical monitoring service and must not be marketed as one. No vital-sign interpretation beyond recording what a consumer device reports. |

## 3. Phase 2 services (months 6–12, gated)

### 3.1 Dementia-friendly support — `dementia-support`

Non-clinical support for clients with mild to moderate cognitive change: routine anchoring, cueing, redirection, safe-walking, meal and hydration prompts, caregiver respite, and environmental simplification.

**Gate:** at least two caregivers complete a recognized dementia care training program; a written behavioral-support protocol exists; the RN supervisor and administrator sign off. **Never** call this "memory care," which implies a licensed facility program.

### 3.2 Post-discharge support — `hospital-to-home`

A flat three-day non-clinical bridge: pickup and ride home, pharmacy and grocery run, meal setup, fall-hazard clearing, medication reminder setup, 24- and 72-hour check-in calls, and a written summary to the family and the referring discharge planner.

**Gate:** a written coordination protocol with at least one hospital or skilled home health partner; a HIPAA-aware information-handling process; a same-day triage workflow.

**Boundary:** no clinical assessment, no wound care, no medication administration, no change to the home health plan of care.

### 3.3 Family caregiver coaching — `family-caregiver-coaching`

A 90-minute paid planning session with a written action plan, a local resource map, and an optional 30-day check-in. Education and planning only — no legal, financial, or clinical advice.

**Gate:** a trained facilitator; a vetted resource list; a clear disclaimer; professional liability review.

### 3.4 Home safety & aging-in-place review — `home-safety-assessment`

A room-by-room non-clinical walkthrough with a prioritized written report and cost ranges for recommended changes.

**Gate:** a standardized checklist; a referral list of licensed contractors; errors-and-omissions coverage review. No construction or medical advice.

## 4. Phase 3 services (year 2+, gated)

### 4.1 Overnight & 24-hour care — `overnight-care`

Awake overnight coverage and continuous care for clients who cannot be left alone.

**Gate:** an overnight staffing bench (minimum four trained caregivers), fatigue-management policy, enhanced supervision, higher workers' compensation classification, and a rate that reflects true labor cost. **Do not advertise 24/7 coverage before the bench exists.**

### 4.2 Live-in care

**Gate:** legal review of live-in employment, wage-and-hour rules, sleeping-time compensation, and housing standards. High compliance risk for a small agency; defer unless there is clear demand.

### 4.3 Medicaid CCC Plus contracting

**Gate:** DMAS provider enrollment, Medicaid-specific billing systems, licensure in good standing, and a compliance program. Medicaid reimbursement is typically lower than private pay and requires volume. Decide deliberately, not by accident.

## 5. Services deliberately not offered

| Service | Why not |
|---|---|
| Skilled nursing | Requires Medicare certification, clinical leadership, and a different operating model |
| Physical / occupational / speech therapy | Licensed clinical scope; refer to Medicare-certified agencies |
| Wound care | Skilled nursing task |
| Medication administration | Regulated nursing task; reminders only |
| IV therapy | Skilled nursing task |
| Hospice care | Requires a hospice license; partner with hospice providers |
| Memory care | Implies a licensed facility program |
| Medical alert monitoring | Regulated and better bought from a specialist vendor |
| Heavy house cleaning / yard work | Outside scope; refer to licensed contractors |
| Home repair / construction | Contractor licensing and liability |
| Emergency response | 911 is the answer; the website must say so plainly |

## 7. Task-list discipline

Each service must have a one-page task list that:

1. Lists every task the caregiver may perform.
2. Lists what the caregiver must not do.
3. Is signed by the client or responsible party at intake.
4. Is reviewed at every 90-day care-plan reassessment.
5. Is used in caregiver training and competency checks.

Without this, "scope creep" becomes a licensing problem. A caregiver who "just helps with the pills" has crossed into nursing.
