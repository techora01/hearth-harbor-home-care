# Value-Added Services — What Actually Creates Loyalty

**Prepared:** 23 November 2026
**Purpose:** Define the non-hourly services that differentiate Hearth & Harbor, with realistic cost, risk, and launch gating.

---

## 1. Why value-added services matter

Hourly care is a commodity. Two agencies can charge $36/hour, employ similar caregivers, and be indistinguishable on an invoice. Loyalty comes from the things around the hour:

- How fast the phone is answered.
- Whether the family understands what happened.
- Whether a crisis is handled calmly.
- Whether the agency helps with the paperwork nobody explains.

These are cheap to deliver well and expensive to deliver badly. Each one below has an owner and a written standard.

---

## 2. The core value-added set

### 2.1 The Friday Family Brief

**What it is:** a short written update every Friday — one meaningful observation, what was done, what changed, what is coming next week, and one thing the family should watch.

**Format:** email by default; print for families without email; SMS link as an option. Under 150 words. Never a generic checklist.

**Why it works:** it converts invisible labor into visible care. It also forces the caregiver and coordinator to document honestly. Families cite communication as a top reason for staying with an agency.

**Cost:** 10–15 minutes of coordinator time per client per week. At 40 clients, that is roughly 7–10 coordinator hours per week.

**Owner:** Care Coordinator.
**Standard:** sent by 4 p.m. Friday; 10% audited weekly for specificity; a missed brief is a service incident.

**Risk:** becomes a template nobody reads. Mitigation: keep it human, lead with one sentence a family could not get from a timesheet, and ask quarterly whether it is still useful.

---

### 2.2 The named coordinator and direct line

**What it is:** one named human owns the case, with a direct phone number and published office hours. A second coordinator is named as backup.

**Why it works:** the single most common complaint about large agencies is being routed through a call center. A name and a number are a promise a franchise cannot easily match locally.

**Cost:** coordinator capacity planning — roughly 35–45 active clients per full-time coordinator.

**Owner:** Client Services Lead.
**Standard:** calls returned within one business hour during office hours; after-hours voicemail triaged by 9 a.m. the next business day; emergency instructions on the voicemail greeting.

**Risk:** the coordinator becomes a single point of failure. Mitigation: documented case notes, a named backup, and a rule that no coordinator takes leave without a handover.

---

### 2.3 The trained backup caregiver

**What it is:** every client meets a backup caregiver before the primary caregiver is unavailable. The backup has the care plan, the home layout, and the client's preferences.

**Why it works:** the fear of "who will come if she's sick" is universal. A real backup converts an emergency into a routine substitution.

**Cost:** a staffing bench of at least 1.3 caregivers per active client, or a paid flex pool. This is the most expensive promise in the catalog.

**Owner:** Scheduling Lead.
**Standard:** backup introduced within the first 14 days; backup coverage confirmed within 2 hours for a same-day callout; no uncovered shift without administrator notification.

**Risk:** promising a backup before the bench exists. Mitigation: publish the policy only when the bench is real, and audit coverage monthly.

---

### 2.4 The 72-hour hospital-to-home bridge

**What it is:** a flat-fee, non-clinical package for the first three days after discharge: ride home, pharmacy and groceries, meal setup, fall-hazard clearing, medication reminder setup, 24- and 72-hour calls, and a written summary to the family and discharge planner.

**Why it works:** discharge is the highest-urgency, highest-referral moment in the care journey. Medicare covers skilled home health but not custodial or companion care [S22]. The bridge fills a real gap without competing with clinical providers.

**Price:** $395 for the package (illustrative), with optional ongoing care.

**Cost:** 6–9 caregiver hours plus coordination. Margin is thin but the referral value is high.

**Owner:** Intake Lead.
**Standard:** same-day triage during business hours; written scope confirmation before the first visit; no clinical tasks; coordination documented with the discharging team.

**Risk:** scope creep into clinical territory, or a hospital expecting services the agency cannot provide. Mitigation: a one-page scope sheet for discharge planners and a scripted intake.

---

### 2.5 The home safety and aging-in-place review

**What it is:** a room-by-room non-clinical walkthrough with a prioritized written report and cost ranges.

**Why it works:** it is a low-commitment paid entry offer that demonstrates competence, and it reduces fall risk for existing clients.

**Price:** $149 flat (illustrative); free for clients who commit to 20+ hours/week.

**Owner:** Care Coordinator with a trained assessor.
**Standard:** 60–90 minutes on site; report within 48 hours; three priority tiers (now / 30 days / consider); referrals to licensed contractors only.

**Risk:** implying that a review prevents all falls, or drifting into construction advice. Mitigation: written disclaimer and contractor referral list.

---

### 2.6 Family caregiver coaching

**What it is:** a 90-minute paid planning session with a written action plan, a local resource map, and an optional 30-day check-in.

**Why it works:** many families are not ready to hire care. Coaching builds trust, creates a pipeline, and supports the respite line.

**Price:** $225 (illustrative).

**Owner:** Family Services Lead.
**Standard:** structured intake form before the session; written plan within 72 hours; referrals to legal, financial, and clinical professionals where appropriate.

**Risk:** giving legal, financial, or clinical advice. Mitigation: a clear scope statement and a referral-first posture.

---

### 2.7 The local resource map

**What it is:** a one-page, printed and digital map of Prince William County resources: Area Agency on Aging, Meals on Wheels, senior centers, caregiver support groups, transportation, elder law, and adult protective services.

**Why it works:** it is genuinely useful, it positions the agency as a local expert, and it is cheap to produce.

**Cost:** a few hours of research and design; reprinted quarterly.

**Owner:** Marketing Lead.
**Standard:** verified phone numbers and hours; dated version; no paid placements.

**Risk:** stale information. Mitigation: a quarterly review owner and a version date on every copy.

---

### 2.8 The family document kit

**What it is:** a simple folder — physical or digital — containing the care plan, the medication list template, emergency contacts, advance directive location, insurance information, and a one-page "what to do in an emergency" sheet.

**Why it works:** families are overwhelmed by paperwork. A single organized kit reduces chaos and makes the agency indispensable.

**Cost:** under $5 per client for print; near zero for digital.

**Owner:** Care Coordinator.
**Standard:** completed at intake; reviewed at every 90-day reassessment; stored securely.

**Risk:** mishandling protected health information. Mitigation: encryption, access controls, and a retention policy.

---

### 2.9 The quarterly family check-in call

**What it is:** a scheduled 20-minute call with the responsible family member every quarter, separate from the weekly brief.

**Why it works:** it catches dissatisfaction before it becomes a cancellation, and it surfaces changing needs early.

**Owner:** Client Services Lead.
**Standard:** scheduled in advance; three questions (what is working, what is not, what is changing); documented actions.

---

### 2.10 The caregiver introduction visit

**What it is:** a short, paid or complimentary visit where the caregiver and backup meet the client and family before care formally begins.

**Why it works:** it addresses the "stranger in the house" objection directly and reduces first-week cancellations.

**Cost:** 30–45 minutes of caregiver time.

**Owner:** Scheduling Lead.
**Standard:** offered to every new client; documented in the care plan.

## 3. What not to add

| Idea | Why it fails the test |
|---|---|
| A custom mobile app | Expensive, poorly adopted by older adults, distracts from the human promise |
| A branded wearable | Regulated, support-heavy, and better bought from a specialist |
| A "care concierge" tier | Vague, hard to price, and signals luxury rather than reliability |
| A referral rewards program | Inducement and fraud-and-abuse risk |
| A monthly newsletter with clinical tips | Creates clinical liability without adding trust |
| A "guaranteed 15-minute arrival" promise | Unkeepable in Northern Virginia traffic; erodes credibility |

## 4. Cost and capacity model

| Value-added service | Annual cost at 30 clients | Revenue (if paid) | Retention value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Friday Family Brief | ~$9,000 (coordinator time) | $0 | High |
| Named coordinator | Embedded in coordinator role | $0 | Very high |
| Trained backup | ~$12,000 (bench premium) | $0 | High |
| Hospital bridge | ~$3,000 (subsidized) | ~$12,000 | Very high (referrals) |
| Home safety review | ~$2,000 | ~$4,500 | Medium |
| Family coaching | ~$2,500 | ~$5,400 | Medium |
| Resource map | ~$1,500 | $0 | Medium |
| Document kit | ~$900 | $0 | Medium |
| Quarterly check-in | ~$3,000 | $0 | High |
| Caregiver introduction | ~$1,500 | $0 | High |

**Total:** roughly $35,000 per year in direct cost at 30 clients, or about $97 per client per month. That is roughly 2.7 hours of billed care per client per month at $36/hour.

**Interpretation:** the value-added program is affordable if it prevents even one avoided cancellation per month (a lost client is worth roughly $22,500/year at 12 hours/week). The program pays for itself through retention and referrals, not through direct fees.

---

## 5. Launch sequence

| When | Add | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Named coordinator, Friday brief, caregiver introduction, document kit | Zero or low cost; highest trust impact |
| Month 2 | Trained backup, quarterly check-in | Requires bench and coordinator capacity |
| Month 3 | Hospital bridge, resource map | Requires referral partner and verified content |
| Month 4 | Home safety review | Requires checklist, training, and insurance review |
| Month 6 | Family coaching | Requires facilitator training and liability review |

**Rule:** do not launch a value-added service until its owner, standard, and cost are written down. A promise without an owner is a future complaint.

---

## 6. How to measure whether it works

| Metric | Target | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Friday brief open/read rate | ≥70% | Email platform or follow-up call |
| 90-day client retention | ≥85% | Billing system |
| Referral source repeat rate | ≥3 repeat sources in 90 days | Intake log |
| Same-day callout coverage | ≥95% covered within 2 hours | Scheduling log |
| Family satisfaction (quarterly) | ≥4.5/5 | Check-in survey |
| Complaint resolution time | ≤48 hours | Complaint log |

If a value-added service does not move one of these metrics within two quarters, retire it. Complexity is a cost.
