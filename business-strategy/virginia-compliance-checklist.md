# Virginia Compliance Checklist — Home Care Organization Launch

**Prepared:** 23 November 2026
**Business:** Hearth & Harbor Home Care (concept)
**Jurisdiction:** Commonwealth of Virginia · Prince William County
**Primary regulator:** Virginia Department of Health, Office of Licensure and Certification (VDH OLC)

> **Not legal advice.** This is a planning checklist built from public regulations and agency guidance. Regulations change. Every item must be verified against the current Virginia Administrative Code and confirmed with qualified Virginia counsel before filing or delivering care. Nothing here should be relied on as a legal opinion.

---

## 0. The decision that drives everything

Virginia licenses **home care organizations (HCOs)** that provide home health services, personal care services, or pharmaceutical services in a person's residence [S4][S5]. Companion, homemaker, and chore services **alone** may be exempt under 12VAC5-381-30 [S6].

**Therefore there are two lawful launch paths:**

| Path | Services | Licensure | Speed | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Exempt companion/homemaker agency** | Companion, homemaker, chore, companionship, errands — **no hands-on personal care** | File for exemption (written request + $75 fee) [S6][S7] | Fast | Low regulatory, but lower revenue per client and limited differentiation |
| **B. Licensed home care organization** | Companion **plus** personal care (bathing, dressing, toileting, transfers, feeding) | Full HCO license ($1,500 application fee) [S7] | 3–9 months realistically | Higher compliance burden, but the core service families need |

**Recommendation:** Path B. Personal care is the service that produces real revenue and real referrals. But **never deliver personal care before the license is issued** — not "while the application is pending," not "as a favor." An unlicensed personal care visit is a violation and an insurance problem.

**Critical trap:** an "exempt" companion agency that begins helping with bathing because a client asked has voided its exemption. Scope discipline is a legal requirement, not a preference.

### 2.2 Administrator qualification (12VAC5-381-170)

The governing body must appoint an administrator with:

- **At least one year of training and experience in direct health care service delivery**, and
- **At least one year within the last five years of supervisory or administrative management experience in home health care or a related health program** [S8].

The administrator is responsible for day-to-day management, including organizing administrative functions, maintaining liaison with the governing body, employing qualified personnel, ensuring orientation/training/evaluation, ensuring the accuracy of public information materials, and implementing budgeting and accounting systems [S8].

**Planning consequence:** this is the most common launch bottleneck. A capable operator without a clinical background may not qualify. Options: hire a qualified administrator, partner with an experienced operator, or document qualifying experience carefully. Do not overstate credentials on the application.

### 2.3 Supervising registered nurse

Personal care requires clinical supervision. The agency needs a written agreement with a Virginia-licensed RN who:

- Reviews and approves personal care plans.
- Supervises personal care aides.
- Conducts or delegates competency validation.
- Is available for consultation and periodic reassessment.

**Planning consequence:** budget for 4–10 RN hours per week at launch, scaling with census. This is a real cost that many new agencies underestimate.

## 4. Policies and procedures manual — required contents

At minimum, the manual should cover:

1. **Client rights and responsibilities**, including dignity, privacy, choice, and the complaint process.
2. **Admission and eligibility**, including a non-clinical assessment and a determination that needs can be safely met.
3. **Care plan development, review, and revision**, with client/family participation and 90-day reassessment.
4. **Service scope and task lists**, including explicit prohibitions on clinical tasks.
5. **Medication reminder protocol**, distinguishing reminders from administration.
6. **Infection control and standard precautions.**
7. **Emergency and disaster plan**, including 911 protocol, evacuation, and weather events.
8. **Incident reporting and investigation**, including abuse, neglect, exploitation, falls, medication errors, and missing persons.
9. **Mandated reporting** of suspected abuse/neglect of adults and children under Virginia law.
10. **Complaint intake, investigation, and resolution**, with timelines and records.
11. **Personnel** — hiring, orientation, training, competency, supervision, evaluation, discipline, termination.
12. **Criminal records checks and sworn disclosures.**
13. **Confidentiality and HIPAA/privacy practices**, including minimum necessary use, authorized disclosures, and breach response.
14. **Record retention and destruction**, including the Virginia retention period for client records.
15. **Supervision and case review**, including RN oversight of personal care.
16. **Continuity of care and backup staffing.**
17. **Client funds and property**, if the agency ever handles either (avoid at launch).
18. **Transportation safety**, including insurance, seat belts, and prohibited conditions.
19. **Cultural competence and language access.**
20. **Quality improvement**, including audits, satisfaction surveys, and corrective action.

**Rule:** the manual must describe what the agency actually does. A beautiful manual that does not match practice is worse than a plain one that does, because it proves knowledge of the requirement and intent to violate it.

## 5. Insurance and bonding (12VAC5-381-210)

The governing body must ensure the organization **and its contractors** have appropriate indemnity coverage, and must purchase and maintain at all times [S10]:

| # | Coverage | Minimum | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Malpractice insurance | Consistent with § 8.01-581.15 of the Code of Virginia [S10] | ☐ |
| 5.2 | Comprehensive general liability | **At least $1 million per occurrence**, covering personal property damage, bodily injury, product liability, libel and slander [S10] | ☐ |
| 5.3 | Third-party crime insurance or blanket fidelity bond | **At least $50,000** [S10] | ☐ |
| 5.4 | Workers' compensation | Virginia statutory requirement for employees | ☐ |
| 5.5 | Commercial auto / non-owned auto | If caregivers drive clients or run errands | ☐ |
| 5.6 | Professional liability / errors and omissions | For care coordination, assessments, and coaching | ☐ |
| 5.7 | Cyber liability | Recommended given health information and online intake | ☐ |
| 5.8 | Employment practices liability | Recommended as headcount grows | ☐ |
| 5.9 | Umbrella / excess liability | Recommended; $1M–$3M | ☐ |

**Verification:** obtain certificates of insurance naming the agency, confirm additional-insured status where appropriate, and calendar renewal dates. Do not accept a caregiver's personal auto policy as coverage for transporting clients.

| 8.1 | Service agreement | Scope, exclusions, rates, minimums, premiums, cancellation, termination, notice, privacy, complaint process, emergency procedures |

| 8.4 | Financial agreement | Rate schedule, billing cycle, payment terms, returned-payment fee, late fee |

| 8.6 | Complaint procedure | How to complain, to whom, timelines, and the state complaint route |
| 8.7 | Bill of rights | Client rights in plain language, available in Spanish if serving Spanish-speaking families |

## 12. Launch gate — do not open until all are true

- [ ] Entity formed, EIN obtained, bank account open.
- [ ] Prince William BPOL and any town license obtained.
- [ ] VDH HCO license **issued** (if delivering personal care) or exemption **granted** (if companion only).
- [ ] Administrator meets 12VAC5-381-170 qualifications and is documented.
- [ ] Supervising RN agreement signed.
- [ ] Insurance bound: malpractice, $1M general liability, $50,000 crime/fidelity bond, workers' comp, auto.
- [ ] Policy manual adopted and staff trained.
- [ ] Criminal records check process live; no uncleared caregiver has client contact.
- [ ] Service agreement, care plan, privacy notice, and complaint procedure finalized.
- [ ] Scheduling, billing, and after-hours coverage operational.
- [ ] Website claims match actual licensure, insurance, and staffing.
- [ ] At least two caregivers fully onboarded and competency-validated.
- [ ] At least one referral partner briefed on scope.
- [ ] Emergency and incident protocols tested with a tabletop exercise.

**If any box is unchecked, the agency is not ready. Marketing can wait; a licensing violation cannot be undone.**

## 14. Open questions for counsel

1. Does our exact service mix require HCO licensure, or does any part qualify for the 12VAC5-381-30 exemption?
2. Are we a HIPAA covered entity or a business associate, and what is the basis?
3. What are the Virginia record-retention requirements for client and personnel records?
4. How should we structure the administrator role if the owner does not meet 12VAC5-381-170?
5. What are the wage-and-hour rules for overnight shifts and travel time in Virginia?
6. What disclosures and consents are required for the hospital-to-home bridge?
7. Does the Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act apply to our marketing list and website analytics?
8. What are the requirements for EVV if we later enroll in Medicaid?
