# Business Positioning — Heart & Harbor Health & Medical Support

**Prepared:** 23 November 2026
**Status:** Recommended positioning for launch. All business identifiers remain placeholders until licensure and legal review.

---

## 1. The one-sentence position

> **Heart & Harbor Health & Medical Support is the communication-first home care agency for Prince William County: a named coordinator, a trained backup, published rates, and a written family update every week.**

That sentence is the business. Everything else — services, website, brochures, scripts — exists to make it credible and repeatable.

---

## 2. Why this position

The local market is crowded but undifferentiated. Agencies compete with near-identical language: compassionate, trusted, affordable, family-first. Families cannot evaluate those claims before signing, so they default to brand recognition or the lowest quoted rate.

The research points to three durable frustrations:

1. **Uncertainty about who will actually arrive.** Caregiver consistency is the most visible quality signal, and it is rarely guaranteed.
2. **Silence after care begins.** Families want to know what happened, not just that a visit was billed.
3. **Opaque pricing and contracts.** Many agencies quote only after an assessment and bury premiums in the agreement.

A small agency cannot outspend a franchise. It can out-operate one on these three dimensions.

## 4. Target customer

### Primary buyer
**"The coordinating daughter."** Age 42–60. Lives in Prince William, Fairfax, or another state. Works full time. Manages a parent's appointments, medications, and siblings. She is not looking for the cheapest option; she is looking for the option that will not create more work for her.

### Primary user
**The older adult who wants to stay home.** Age 72–90. May resist "a stranger in the house." Values routine, privacy, and being asked rather than managed. The caregiver match matters more than the brochure.

### Secondary buyers
- A spouse providing full-time care who needs respite.
- A hospital discharge planner arranging the first week home.
- A long-term care insurance policyholder who needs a documented provider.
- A Spanish-speaking family that has struggled to find a coordinator who can explain the plan.

### Explicit non-targets (year one)
- Skilled nursing or therapy referrals (partner instead).
- Clients needing 24/7 continuous care at launch (operationally risky).
- Facility-based staffing contracts.
- Medicaid waiver clients before DMAS enrollment.

## 6. Messaging hierarchy

**Level 1 — Promise:** Care that keeps home within reach.
**Level 2 — Differentiator:** The clearest way to care for a parent at home.
**Level 3 — Proof:** Named coordinator · trained backup · published rates · weekly family brief.
**Level 4 — Services:** Companion care, personal care, respite, homemaker, transportation, medication reminders, wellness checks.
**Level 5 — Local proof:** Ten communities, one county, hospital-to-home bridge.
**Level 6 — Action:** Book a 15-minute call.

## 7. Tone of voice

- **Calm, not alarmist.** Never imply that a parent is in imminent danger to force a sale.
- **Specific, not sentimental.** "A written update every Friday" beats "peace of mind."
- **Honest about limits.** Saying "we do not provide nursing" builds more trust than implying it.
- **Respectful of the older adult.** Use "client," not "patient." Ask before deciding.
- **Plain English.** Short sentences. Define terms like "activities of daily living" and "home health."
- **Bilingual by design, not by machine.** Human translation for Spanish materials; no machine-translated care instructions.

**Words to use:** reliable, named, written, local, trained, supervised, transparent, professional, coordinated.
**Words to avoid:** compassionate (overused), affordable (unprovable), elite, concierge, medical, nursing, guaranteed, cure, best.

## 8. Elevator pitches

**15 seconds**
"We're a local home care agency in Prince William County. You get one named coordinator, a trained backup, and a written update every week. Rates are published, and there's no long-term contract."

**30 seconds**
"Most families call us after a fall or a hospital stay. We do a home visit, write a care plan, and match a caregiver to your parent's routine and language. You always know who's coming and what they did. We're professional — no nursing or therapy — so we work alongside the clinical team rather than replacing it."

**Referral-partner version**
"We're the agency that sends the family a written update every Friday and publishes its rates. If you have a patient going home who needs non-clinical support in the first 72 hours, we can triage same-day and coordinate with the discharging team. We don't provide skilled care, so we won't compete with your clinical referrals."

## 9. Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "You're new. Why should I trust you?" | "You shouldn't trust a brochure. Ask for our license status, insurance certificate, background-check policy, and a sample care plan. We'll send all four before you decide." |
| "You're more expensive than a registry." | "A registry caregiver is an independent contractor you hire, supervise, and insure. We employ, train, background-check, supervise, and back up our caregivers. The hourly rate includes that infrastructure." |
| "I don't want a stranger in my house." | "That's the right instinct. We introduce the caregiver and a backup before care starts, and we change the match at 72 hours if it isn't working." |
| "Medicare will cover it." | "Medicare covers short-term skilled home health ordered by a doctor. It generally does not cover custodial or companion care. We'll help you understand what your policy or benefits actually cover." |
| "We only need two hours." | "Our minimum is four hours because travel, documentation, and supervision are built into every visit. Four hours also gives the caregiver time to do the job without rushing." |
| "Can you give her pills?" | "We can remind and document. We cannot administer medication — that's a nursing task. We'll coordinate with the pharmacy and the prescriber." |

## 10. Visual identity

**Core palette:** deep teal (#0F766E) for trust and calm; warm sand (#E9D8BD) for home; ink (#10202A) for legibility; muted sage (#8FAE9E) for care; terracotta (#C97B5E) as a human accent.

**Typography:** a warm humanist sans for body (Source Sans 3 / Inter) and a restrained serif or geometric sans for headlines, depending on the channel. Avoid script fonts and clinical blues alone.

**Photography/illustration direction:** real homes, natural light, hands doing ordinary things, older adults with agency and dignity. No sad, isolated figures; no white coats; no stock "doctor pointing at clipboard." The demo sites use original vector illustrations because generated photography was unavailable, and to avoid licensing and consent issues.

**Logo:** a hearth arch containing a heart, over a harbor wave. Nine lockup variations are implemented in the demo builds.

**Accessibility:** minimum 16px body text, 4.5:1 contrast for body copy, 44px touch targets, visible focus, reduced-motion support. Older adults are the user; accessibility is market fit, not compliance theater.

## 11. What we are not

- We are not a home health agency and do not provide skilled nursing, therapy, or wound care.
- We are not a registry or a marketplace.
- We are not a facility or a memory care unit.
- We are not a Medicaid provider at launch.
- We are not a 24/7 continuous-care operation at launch.
- We are not the cheapest option, and we will not pretend to be.

Saying these things clearly is part of the brand. Families are used to being oversold.

---

## 12. Positioning test

Before publishing any marketing asset, ask:

1. Does it name a **specific operational behavior**, not just an adjective?
2. Would a competitor be **uncomfortable** publishing the same claim because it requires real work?
3. Can a family **verify** it during the first two weeks?
4. Does it avoid implying **clinical services or credentials** we do not have?
5. Does it respect the older adult's **dignity and choice**?

If the answer to 1, 3, or 4 is no, rewrite it.
