## 3. Service page formula

Every service page follows the same structure:

1. **H1:** the service name, plain.
2. **Lede:** one sentence on who it is for and what it changes.
3. **Who it is for:** specific situations, not demographics.
4. **The problem it solves:** the honest friction.
5. **What a visit includes:** a task list families can check.
6. **What it costs:** rate and minimum, with a link to pricing.
7. **Regulatory notes:** scope and supervision, in plain language.
8. **Business case / what to expect:** realistic expectations.
9. **CTA:** ask about this service.

**Rule:** no page may imply clinical capability. "Personal care" means ADLs, not nursing.

## 4. Location page formula

1. **H1:** Home care in [Community], Virginia.
2. **Lede:** local context in one or two sentences.
3. **What families here usually ask for.**
4. **Local context:** landmarks, housing stock, hospitals, senior centers.
5. **Services available in [Community].**
6. **How coverage works:** travel radius, start times, honest limits.
7. **ZIP codes served.**
8. **Nearby communities.**
9. **Local resources:** Area Agency on Aging, Eldercare Locator.
10. **CTA:** check availability.

**Rule:** do not publish a location page for a community the agency cannot realistically staff.

Subject: Your call with Hearth & Harbor
Body: Thank you for reaching out. Here is what happens next, who your coordinator is, and how to reach us. Attach the resource map.

Subject: A plain answer about what Medicare covers

**Email 5 — day 14**
Subject: Still deciding? That's normal.
Body: Offer a 15-minute call with no assessment. Leave the door open.

**Unsubscribe and consent:** every marketing email includes an unsubscribe link and a physical address. Transactional follow-up for an active inquiry is separate from marketing consent.

## 11. Brand voice rules

**Do:**
- Lead with a specific behavior.
- Use short sentences and plain words.
- Name the limitation before the benefit.
- Respect the older adult's agency.
- Write for a tired person reading on a phone.

**Do not:**
- Use "compassionate" as a substitute for evidence.
- Claim "affordable," "elite," "concierge," or "best."
- Imply clinical capability.
- Use fear to force a decision.
- Invent testimonials, staff, or credentials.
- Machine-translate care instructions.
